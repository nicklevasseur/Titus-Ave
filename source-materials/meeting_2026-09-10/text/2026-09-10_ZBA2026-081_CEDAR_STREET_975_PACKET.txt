Variance Application
975 Cedar Street, Manchester, New Hampshire
BACKGROUND:
The owner of record and applicant for this variance request is The JEN Homestead Revocable
Trust, c/o John & Elaine Niemiec, Trustees, of 975 Cedar Street in Manchester, New
Hampshire. The subject properties are Lot 6A, 15 and 16 on Manchester Tax Map 379. The
parcels are located between Cedar and Auburn Streets and Tarrytown Road and Canton Street.
Lot 6A has 126’ of frontage along Cedar Street and an area of 12,540 SF. Lot 15 has 200’ of
frontage along Auburn Street and a parcel area of 20,062 SF. Lot 16 has 50’ of frontage along
Auburn Street and an area of 5,003 SF. There is a 2-story single family dwelling on lot 6A,
which is the Niemiec residence and a 1-1/2-story single family dwelling on Lot 16. Lot 15 is
undeveloped other than some accessory structures. City records indicate the house on Lot 6A
was built in 1985 and the house on Lot 16 was built in 1923. The parcels are located in the
MX-1 zoning district which requires 50’ of lot width, 5,000 SF of lot area, a 10’ minimum and
20’ maximum front setback, 10’ side setback (one side can be reduced to 5’ if the total is 20’)
and a rear setback of 20’ for single-family houses, The Niemiec’s have resided at 975 Cedar
Street since the house was built. The facade of their residence faces Cedar Street and they use
lot 15 as their back yard. The applicant wants to adjust the lot lines of the parcels as shown on
the plan. The end result would be increasing the size of the Auburn Street parcel and annexing
the remainder of lot 379-15 onto the Cedar Street house, to match how the land is used.
During the zoning review, staff noted some unpermitted improvements on the parcels. The
applicant has since pulled after the fact permits but zoning relief is needed for 5 sheds and 2
overhead covers. Unfortunately, when we add lot 379-15 onto lot 379-6A, it creates a through
lot and you cannot use the accessory structure setbacks on through lots, If approved, the
applicant wishes to maintain five of the accessory structures with setbacks between 0.9’ to 5.6’
and relocate one shed and one lean-to 4’+ off the sideline. In addition, the larger shed is
approximately 0.8’ higher than the 15’ maximum allowed and the shed along Auburn Street is
considered in the front yard. There is an existing 5’ tall stockade fence along the frontage of lot
379-15 which functions as a rear yard to the house. While the accessory structures do not
comply with the required side yard setback, this is how the yard has been used for years
without any complaint or enforcement. The properties are well maintained and fit in well with
the neighborhood.
SECTIONS OF THE ZONING ORDINANCE NEEDING RELIEF:
LOT 379-6A:
Section 5.3.1(A).3.C - Maintain shed 3.8’ from a front lot line
Section 5.3.1(A).3.E - Maintain lean-to 0.9’ from a side lot line
Section 5.3.1(A).3.E - Maintain shed 1.0’ from a side lot line
Section 5.3.1(A).3.E - 2 counts of relocated sheds 4’, or more from a side lot line
Section 5.3.1(A).3.E - Maintain shed 5.0’ from a side lot line
Section 5.3.1(A).3.E - Maintain shed 5.6’ from a side lot line
Section 5.6.2(B) - Maintain 16’ high shed where 15’ is allowed
1. Granting the variance would not be contrary to the public interest because:

Approval of this variance request would allow the applicant to adjust the lot lines of

their parcels and maintain their yard as they have for years. While this proposal would

result in Lot 6A becoming a through lot, that is how the owners have historically used

their land. The lot is screened from Auburn Street (paid for by the Elliott) and the

majority of the accessory structures are not visible from the road. The applicant will
===== page 19 =====
relocate two of the existing accessory structures to be at least 4’ from the side lot line
and maintain the other ones as is. The accessory structure that is 0.9’ from the side lot
line is a roof for an existing pool filtration system and is not possible to move without
moving the mechanical and plumbing systems. One of the accessory structures, located
5.6’ from the side lot line, is approximately 1’ taller than allowed due to the roof pitch.
This shed was purchased by the applicant from the Elliot Hospital and is almost 100
years old so reducing the height would be a challenge. Maintaining the structures on
this large lot poses no threat to public interest. We believe that approval of this
variance would not be contrary to the public interest.

2. Granting the variance would not be contrary to the Spirit of the Zoning Ordinance
because:

The intent of the zoning ordinance is to prevent overcrowding and unsafe development.
The neighborhood has a mixture of older and newer homes that also have accessory
structures similar distances from the lot lines in the side and rear yards. The subject
parcel is a large city lot and the accessory structures are located in what is used as a
large back yard for the owner. We believe this request is reasonable, within the spirit of
the ordinance and should be approved.

3. By granting the variance, substantial justice would be done because:

Approval of these variance requests would allow the applicant to consolidate their
existing properties and maintain their sheds with minor modifications to bring them
closer to compliance with the Zoning Ordinance. The Zoning Ordinance does not allow
accessory structures to be less than the required 10’ side setback on through lots. The
location of the accessory structures will not change, other than the two sheds being
relocated fully onto the subject lot. The 16’ tall shed poses no detriment to the public.
We see no benefit to the public should the variance request be denied. As the loss to
the applicant would greatly outweigh any perceived public gain, it is our opinion that
the statutes specify that the variance should be approved.

4. The proposed use would not diminish surrounding property values because:

The applicant seeks a variance to allow them to maintain their existing sheds in
approximately the same location on a through Iot. The owners consider this space their
rear yard even though the zoning ordinance does not. There are sheds throughout the
neighborhood that appear to be closer to lot lines in the side or rear yards than is
allowed. The height of the accessory structure that exceeds the 15’ maximum allowed is
due to the pitch of the roof and there is no easy way to modify this old structure, It is in
good condition and does not pose an eyesore or safety concern to any abutting
properties. The location of the sheds will not change the character of the neighborhood
going forward and already exist. As such, approval of this variance would not reduce
the value of surrounding properties.

5. Literal enforcement of the provisions of the ordinance would result in unnecessary
hardship if, owing to the following special conditions of the property that distinguish it
from other properties in the area:

(1) No fair and substantial relationship exists between the general public purposes
of the ordinance and the specific application to the Property;
The applicant wishes to complete a lot line adjustment and merger to consolidate three
existing lots of record into two lots. Proposed lot 6A would become a through lot and
===== page 20 =====
need variances to maintain the sheds within a side and front setback but is really used
as a rear yard for 975 Cedar Street. One of the sheds is 16’ high where 15’ is the
maximum allowed. It is our opinion that allowing the applicant to maintain the
accessory structures and merge the lots as requested is a reasonable use of the
Property and as such, no fair and substantial relationship exists between the general
purposes of the zoning ordinance and the specific application to the Property.

(2) The proposed use is reasonable;
Accessory structures are allowed in this district if they are less than 15’ tall and located
more than 4’ from the property line when located in the side or rear yard on a
conventional lot. The proposed lot reconfiguration creates a through lot which does not
allow for the reduced setback, Because the owner has not obtained permits for the
structures when installed, they are not considered grandfathered which is why relief is
needed. As this is a unique situation where the through Jot is not utilized as one, it is
our opinion that the proposed use is reasonable.
