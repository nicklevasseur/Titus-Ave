_ ADDENDUM TO VARIANCE APPLICATION
DOWNTOWN DOG LODGE LLC
800 GOLD STREET
TAX MAP 875, LOT 5
Introduction

The subject property, Tax Map 875, Lot 5, (the “Property”) is a +30,608-square-foot lot
located in the BC-2: Business Corridor District — Regional Center zoning district with frontage on
Gold Street. The Property is improved by a +9,380-square-foot building built in 1969 and most
recently used for automotive repair. The Property is located in a dense commercial area accessed
from South Willow Street. The Applicant is proposing to redevelop the Property and building into
a high-end dog hotel and daycare facility offering three primary services: overnight boarding, dog
daycare/play, and dog training. All three services are drop-off and pick-up based, meaning the
average client will only be on the Property for a few minutes while dropping off or picking up their
dog.

Zoning Ordinance Article 4, Section 4.4.6.C. defines Animal Boarding and/or Kenneling
Facility as “Any structure or premises on which four or more dogs, cats and/or other household
pets are boarded during the day or for overnight stays. Includes ancillary activities such as
breeding, selling, grooming, training, or keeping animals for adoption or sale.” This section also
provides a minimum lot area for this use of 45,000 square feet. See §4.4.6.C.1. As the Property is
+30,608 square feet, variance relief is required.

Variance relief requested from:
Article 4, Section 4.4.6.C.1., Minimum Lot Area
1. The variance would not be contrary to the public interest because:

For a variance to be contrary to the public interest, the proposal has to conflict with the
ordinance so much that it violates the ordinance’s basic zoning objectives. The relevant tests are
(1) whether the proposal will alter the essential character of the neighborhood; and (2) whether it
threatens the public health, safety or welfare. The Property is in a dense commercial area, and is
directly abutted by a NH Liquor & Wine Outlet, Enviro Arbor Solutions, the rear of the Hannaford
supermarket, and an Enterprise Rent-A-Car parking lot. Mercedes-Benz of Manchester is located
across Gold Street from the Property’s entrance. The essential character of the neighborhood is
commercial, with many of the lots and buildings developed in the 1960s, ‘70s, and ‘80s. The
proposed use of the existing building will have nearly no effect on the neighborhood composition
and will not alter the essential character thereof. Similarly, renovating and reviving this building
from an automotive use to the proposed dog daycare will not threaten the public health, safety, or
welfare, but will instead enable use of the Property and add a necessary modern amenity to this
part of Manchester.
===== page 13 =====
2. The spirit of the Ordinance is observed because:

Because it is in the public’s interest to uphold the spirit of the ordinance, the New
Hampshire Supreme Court has held that these two criteria are related. If an application meets the
first criterion, it almost certainly meet the second. See Farrar v. City of Keene, 158 N.H. 684
(2009). Among the stated purposes of the Manchester Zoning Ordinance is to encourage the most
appropriate use of land, and to allow for planned, orderly, and beneficial growth. (§1 .02(H))
Furthermore, the stated purpose of the BC-2 zone “is to provide for medium to high intensity
commercial uses that have a community-wide or regional market and that benefit from access to
major city streets and highways.” (§3.3-A.) The proposed use operates via customers dropping off
and picking up their pets without the need to park for long periods. The Property’s proximity to
South Willow Street and Interstate 293 provide the benefit the BC-2 zone promotes, and this
proposal increases diversity of uses in this neighborhood. Therefore, it complies with the spirit of
the Zoning Ordinance.

3. Substantial justice would be done because:

The New Hampshire Supreme Court has held that measuring substantial justice requires
balancing public and private rights. “Perhaps the only guiding rule is that any loss to the individual
that is not outweighed by a gain to the general public is an injustice.” Harborside Assocs., LP. v.
Parade Residence Hotel, LLC, 162 N.H. 508, 515 (2011). There is no gain to the general public
in denying this variance, as it would prohibit a viable use of the existing building in furtherance of
a size restriction aimed to prevent potential nuisance from kennels located on undersized lots in
susceptible areas such as residential zones. The proposed use is appropriate for the area and would
not harm abutters. See U-Haul Co. of New Hampshire & Vermont v. City of Concord, 122 N.H.
910, 913 (1982). Therefore, “the general public would realize no appreciable gain from denying”
the variance. Id. As such, substantial justice is done by granting the variance.

4. The values of surrounding properties would not be diminished because:

The proposal is of a reasonable size and location and consistent with the other commercial
uses in the neighborhood. Most of the surrounding uses are buffered from the Property by
significant parking lots. The Applicant’s project will not be distinguishable from other commercial
uses in the area and therefore should not have any detrimental impact on the value of surrounding
properties. Adding a new use to the Property will, if anything, improve surrounding property
values because it will demonstrate investment into the Property. There is therefore no basis to
conclude that granting the variance would diminish the value of any of the other properties in the
area.

5. A. Literal enforcement of the provisions of the ordinance would result in unnecessary
hardship if: Owing to the following special conditions of the property that distinguish it from
other properties in the area: :

The Property’s proximity to South Willow Street and Interstate 293, the preexisting
building, and the substantial distance from residential zones and less intense uses are all special
characteristics of the Property.

i. No fair and substantial relationship exists between the general purposes of the
ordinance provisions and the specific application of that provision to the property; and

Historically, requiring dog kennels to be on large lots generally helps reduce noise and odor
for neighbors, provides enough space for animal waste management, and prevents overcrowding.
In this case, operations will take place predominantly inside the building. As part of the renovation,
the Applicant will also be installing an entirely new HVAC system with modern heating and
cooling equipment. Energy Recovery Ventilators, a ventilation system that replaces stale indoor
air with fresh outdoor air, will be incorporated throughout the facility to continuously exchange
indoor air with fresh outdoor air and improve ventilation, air quality, humidity control, and odor
management.

The Applicant is also proposing two relatively small outdoor play yards along the rear left
and right sides of the building. There will not be any kennels, cages, or overnight boarding areas
in either outdoor yard and dogs will never be left unsupervised in the outdoor yards. The yards
will only be used for supervised daycare play in small groups and to allow dogs an area to relieve
themselves. Both outdoor yards will be specifically designed for commercial pet use, and will
feature commercial-grade artificial pet turf installed over a drainage system. The yards are
designed to drain into the sanitary sewer system rather than allowing urine or wash water to
discharge into the soil or stormwater system. This will allow the yards to remain clean and sanitary
and will control odors. The outdoor yards will also be surrounded by an approximately 8-foot-tall
solid composite fence. The fencing is a high-end solid material designed to resemble natural stone
rather than an open chain-link-style fence. In addition to providing security and privacy, the solid
fence will help screen the yards and reduce the transmission of noise to surrounding properties.

As a result of the indoor use and thoughtful outdoor design, the general purposes of the
Ordinance’s 45,000-square-foot requirement can be achieved on the Property without the need for
an additional 15,000 square feet. Animal noises and smells will be contained and controlled, and
area pet owners will be provided with a great option for dog daycare and boarding.

ii. The proposed use is a reasonable one.

Dog kennels are permitted by conditional use permit in the BC-2 zone, indicating the City’s
understanding that this use is reasonable provided certain conditions are met. As stated above, the
proposed use provides a necessary service for the City’s dog owners, has been designed to
===== page 15 =====
minimize effects on surrounding properties, and will revitalize an unused building. These factors
render the proposed use reasonable.
