Exhibit A
Variance Application
374 Thornton Street, Manchester, New Hampshire
BACKGROUND:
The owners of record and applicants for this variance request are Jonathan and Krystal
Venturini of 374 Thornton Street in Manchester, New Hampshire. The subject property is Lot
48 on Manchester Tax Map 389. The parcel is located on the west side of Thornton Street
between Wayne Street and Janelle Avenue. It has 227’ of frontage and a parcel area of 42,157
SF (of which 14,852 SF is buildable). There is an existing 1-1/2-story single family dwelling on
the property which is the owner's residence. City records indicate this building was constructed
in 1911 and the property is located in the MX-1 zoning district. For single family houses, this
district requires 50’ of lot width, 5,000 SF of lot area, a 10’ minimum and 20’ maximum front
setback, 10’ side setback (one side can be reduced to 5’ if the total is 20’) and a rear setback
of 20’. For duplexes, this district requires 60’ of lot width (50’ if alley loaded), 6,000 SF of lot
area (5,000 SF if alley loaded), a 10’ minimum and 20’ maximum front setback, 10’ side
setback (one side can be reduced to 5’ if the total is 20’) and a rear setback of 20’. The
applicant purchased the property in May of 2023 from a family member who purchased the
property in 2006. The existing house and one of the 2 detached garages are located on the
southern part of the lot. There is a second detached garage north of the house. The north and
west parts of the parcel have a relatively steep slope that goes up to Columbus Street. The
applicant would like to subdivide the parcel to create a new 2-family building lot with 121’ of
frontage, and 13,955 SF but only 1,812 SF of buildable area. Although we do not have the
required 6,000 SF of buildable area required by the Zoning Ordinance, we believe that with
proper planning and the use of retaining walls there is sufficient space for the proposed duplex
and the ancillary improvements associated with this use. The existing garage would be
demolished as part of this subdivision and a portion of the driveway to the remaining garage
would be reconstructed. The new lot would need zoning relief for inadequate buildable area but
would meet all the other requirements of the zoning ordinance and would be consistent with
the surrounding area.
The intent of the steep slope section of the ordinance is to prevent creating lots that cannot be
built on and to ensure that any new construction does not create problems for abutters. In this
instance, the proposed building would be located at the toe of the slope so the construction of
a new duplex will not negatively impact the downstream abutters. As part of the subdivision
process, DPW will be reviewing the application and any proposed grading to make sure that the
improvements do not cause any runoff issues onto the street. We have shown preliminary
grading for the rear of the parcel to create a backyard by a series of three retaining walls.
Should the variance be approved, we would finalize a grading plan for the proposed driveway
as part of our application. This is not normally part of the process for a road front subdivision
but we understand this is a unique situation.
SECTIONS OF THE ZONING ORDINANCE NEEDING RELIEF:
LOT 389-48B:
Section 5.3.1(B)3.G — 1,812 SF buildable area where 6,000 SF is required
1. Granting the variance would not be contrary to the public interest because:

Approval of this variance request would allow the applicant to create a new building lot.

The remainder lot would meet all the zoning requirements but the proposed lot would

require a variance for inadequate buildable area. The proposed lot would be larger than

the average lot in the neighborhood despite having limited buildable area. The buildable

area requirement exists to ensure that proposed lots are capable of being developed in
===== page 10 =====
a safe and practical way. We believe that the proposed improvements can be
constructed using retaining walls and drive under garages. As the grading part of this
application will receive further scrutiny during the subdivision process to ensure no
negative consequences, we believe that approval of this variance would not be contrary
to the public interest.

2. Granting the variance would not be contrary to the Spirit of the Zoning Ordinance
because:
The intent of the zoning ordinance is to prevent overcrowding and unsafe development.
The existing neighborhood has a ridge line with a significant grade change. Several lots
at the top and bottom of the ridge are also undersized in terms of buildable area but
were created before the buildable area requirement existed. We believe that the
proposed lot is viable through the use of drive under garages and retaining walls.
Subdividing an existing oversized lot of record into two oversized lots will not change
the character of the neighborhood in any way. Consequently, we believe this request is
reasonable, within the spirit of the ordinance and should be approved.

3. By granting the variance, substantial justice would be done because:

Approval of these variance requests would allow the applicant to create a new duplex
building lot from the undeveloped portion of the property that is consistent with the
zoning and surrounding area. Approval of this variance request and subsequent
subdivision would create two new residential units at a time where dwelling units are in
high demand. The existing lot is oversized and underutilized by the existing single-
family residence. The proposed duplex on the new lot would be in keeping with the
character of the area as there are existing two and three-family residences in the
neighborhood. We see no benefit to the public should the variance request be denied.
As the loss to the applicant would greatly outweigh any perceived public gain, it is our
opinion that the statutes specify that the variance should be approved.

4, The proposed use would not diminish surrounding property values because:

The applicant seeks a variance to allow them to subdivide their property and create one
new MX-1 building lot with less than 6,000 SF of buildable area but similar to other
properties in the neighborhood. The proposed dwelling would be equal or higher in
value of the surrounding area because new houses generally tend to appraise higher.
The value of existing dwelling will not significantly change should the subdivision move
forward. As such, approval of this variance would not reduce the value of surrounding
properties.

5. Literal enforcement of the provisions of the ordinance would result in unnecessary
hardship if, owing to the following special conditions of the property that distinguish it
from other properties in the area:

(1) No fair and substantial relationship exists between the general public purposes
of the ordinance and the specific application to the Property;
The applicant wishes to subdivide the parcel to create one new building lot, leaving the
existing house on a reconfigured remainder parcel. The proposed lot would need a
variance for inadequate buildable area. Although the new lot would lack buildable area
by the current zoning ordinance, it would not be out of character with the surrounding
area.
===== page 11 =====
It is our opinion that allowing the applicant to subdivide his property and develop the
new lot as requested is a reasonable use of the property and as such, no fair and
substantial relationship exists between the general purposes of the zoning ordinance
and the specific application to the Property.

(2) The proposed use is reasonable;
Single-family and duplex residences are allowed uses by right in the MX-1 zoning
district. The applicant is asking for relief from the current zoning ordinance to be
allowed to subdivide the parcel to create a new lot with inadequate buildable area in
order to construct a duplex. We have tried to design the improvements to ensure no
downslope drainage issues and still provide a functioning yard. This will require the use
of retaining walls and garage unders but we feel it is viable. As such, it is our opinion
that the proposed use is reasonable.
