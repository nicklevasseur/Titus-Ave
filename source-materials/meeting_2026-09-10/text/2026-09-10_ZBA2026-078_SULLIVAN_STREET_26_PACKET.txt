PRELIMINARY STATEMENT

The Applicant, Brown Capital, LLC, requests a Variance from Article 5, F (4) (Uses within
Floors and Residential Density) of the Manchester Zoning Ordinance (“MZO”) to allow for
greater density in a multi-family building in the Mixed Use — General (MX-2) zone. This
request is relative to a proposed new multi-family building to be constructed at 26 Sullivan
Street, Manchester, NH 03103, which was historically used as a multi-family building with six
(6) units until it was destroyed by a fire and subsequently demolished. The property is sometimes
referred to as Tax Map Number 190-16 (the “Property” hereafter).

Article 5, F (4) allows for five (5) units minimum and, beyond that, every additional unit
requires 1,000 SF. There is an allowable number of units up to nine (9) units as per the MZO.

Here, the request is to allow for nine (9) units total. However, currently due to the Property’s
constraints there is 8,250 SF of lot area and 5,430 SF of buildable land. Therefore, the Property
is deficient by 4,570 SF of buildable land.

It is worth noting that the slope of the Property is the reason for much of the deficiency as the
Property would only be 1,750 SF short if the buildable area was measured by the lot size (8,250
SF. The previous building was, likewise, deficient at six (6) units without compliant buildable
area SF.

The proposed units will be slightly over 1,000 SF. The proposed building will be three (3)
floors with three (3) units per floor, thus, nine (9) units.

Finally, the surrounding area is comprised of buildings that have several units such as:

1. 8 units at 280 Main St. on 5000 sq ft (same block).
2. 12 units 246 Main St. on 5000 sq ft (same block).

3. 10 units 147 Notre Dame Ave on 13214 sq. ft. (same block).

4. 9 Units 202 Notre Dame Ave on 5000 sq. ft. (across street corner).

5. 9 units 217 Notre Dame Ave on 4620 sq. ft. (1 block up).
Variance Criteria

1. Granting the variance would not be contrary to the public interest because:

The standard for prongs one and two of the variance criteria is whether the requested relief, if
granted, will alter the essential character of the neighborhood or negatively impact the health,
welfare, and safety of the surrounding area and mere conflict with the terms of the ordinance
is insufficient as all variance requests are somewhat averse to an ordinance, hence why the relief
is sought in the first instance. Harborside Associates, L.P. v. Parade Residence Hotel, LLC, 162
N.H. 508 (2011).

Furthermore, it important to note that prong 1 is in the negative. That is to say that it does
not require the Applicant to prove that the proposed use is IN the public interest, but only to
prove that it is NOT CONTRARY TO the public interest.

Character:

If granted, the variance will not alter the essential character of the neighborhood because the
general underlying use proposed, which is a multi-family residential use, is otherwise allowed in
the zone. Generally, most of the residential buildings in the neighborhood are multi-family .
residential buildings.

Here, the proposed building will be new construction and in the style of a multi-family
apartment house. Furthermore, the MZO does allow for up to nine (9) units but, here, the only
short fall being the buildable land SF.

Moreover, there are several multi-family buildings in the area with density above what is
currently allowed by the MZO.

2
===== page 16 =====
This proposal will not alter, in any significant way, what has been present at the Property for
decades, which is multi-family use. As such, the essential character of the neighborhood will
remain the same.

Safety:

There is no evidence that would suggest an approval would have any negative effect on
public health and welfare. The proposed building will not add any significant traffic and there is
sufficient parking along Flaherty Lane to be compliant with the MZO. The Property has city
water and sewer so there is no concern with respect to septic and water well radii or usage
impacting surrounding properties. The proposed use as ‘residential’ is allowed in the zone.

2. Ifthe variance were granted, the spirit of the ordinance would be observed because:

As a matter of law, the analysis for both prongs one and two of the Variance criteria are the
same. As such, the Applicant incorporates and repeats the narrative of Prong 1 (above) and
reiterates the same for Prong 2. Harborside Associates, L.P. v. Parade Residence Hotel, LLC,
162 N.H. 508 (2011).

3. Granting the variance would do substantial justice because:

Perhaps the only guiding rule [on this standard] is that any loss to the individual that is not
outweighed by a gain to the general public is an injustice. Malachy Glen Assocs. v. Town of
Chichester, 155 N.H. 102, 109 (2007).

Here, the loss to the applicant in not approving this variance would far outweigh any benefit
to the general public.

3
===== page 17 =====
The proposal adds housing to the City’s stock at a time when the country is dealing with a
housing shortage. Furthermore, the proposal will breathe a much-needed update into a site that
suffered a fire followed by demolition.

Currently the Property is not generating much tax revenue and this proposal will invigorate
the Property and the area while adding to the tax rolls.

Thus, if denied, the public gains nothing.

4, Ifthe variance were granted, the values of the surrounding properties would not be

diminished because:

What is being proposed will be an improvement for the Property, as well as the surrounding
area. The Applicant desires to invigorate the Property and breathe new life into it.

There is simply no evidence to suggest what is being proposed would have a negative impact
on the property values of the surrounding area.

5. Unnecessary Hardship:

“Hardship,” under NH RSA 674:33, I (b) (1) (A) and (B) is a straight forward three step
analyses;

a. What are the special conditions of the property, if any;

b. ‘No fair and substantial relationship exists between the general public purposes of
the ordinance provision and the specific application of that provision to the
property,’ which can be said another way that if the variance is granted would
it unreasonably frustrate the purpose ordinance; and,

c. Is the proposed use reasonable?

The special conditions (a) are satisfied due to the unique shape of the lot as it has a steep
slope/grade. The highest point of the Property (west side) is approximately 231 feet while the
4
===== page 18 =====
lowest side of the Property (East side) is approximately 203 feet. Thus, a slope of approximately
28 feet over a distance of approximately 100 feet.

Furthermore, the Property was previously used for a six (6) unit building that was non-
conforming under today’s zoning, which is significant evidence demonstrating that dwelling
units in excess of what is allowed under the MZO has not caused any negative effects to the
surrounding area. As noted, there are several multi-family buildings in the immediate area that
have more units than what is allowed under the MZO.

Finally, the proposal is otherwise dimensionally compliant with the MZO, save for one other
variance for a retaining wall.

Next is whether “[n]o fair and substantial relationship exists between the general public
purposes of the ordinance provision and the specific application of that provision to the
property.” See NH RSA 674:33; et seg. Or, again, if the variance is granted will it
unreasonably frustrate the purpose of the MZO.

The purpose and goal of the Ordinance provision at issue is to foster more multi-family uses
and mixed uses for a more urbanized area of the City. However, at the same time, the
restrictions as to density are intended to avoid over-crowding.

As such, as a general rule the City would like to see more housing to combat the City’s
struggles with an unhoused population, as well as affordability. This proposal does add housing
that is intended to be market-rate affordable to the ‘masses.’ Although the Applicant is not
proposing workforce housing, per se, but is also not proposing something ‘luxury’ that only a
few would be able to afford.

The Property can easily sustain nine (9) units and there is compliant parking.

5

With the previous building demolished after a fire, the stage is set to utilize this Property
with its convenient location to downtown Manchester, to create newly constructed housing and
maximizing its density without overly taxing the area and/or causing any threat to the public’s
health, safety, and welfare.

Overall, we contend that what the Applicant is asking for with respect to this relief will not
unreasonably frustrate the purpose of the MZO.

As for (c), the proposed use is a residential use and the applicable zone allows for such
residential uses, thus, the proposed use is reasonable.
