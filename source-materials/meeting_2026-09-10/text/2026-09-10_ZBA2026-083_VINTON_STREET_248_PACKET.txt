1. The variance would not be contrary to the public interest because:
a. There are two parts to the request for a deck setback encroachment variance:
1. The first deck variance remedies a stipulation in a 2004 front deck
permit requiring a subsequent variance.
2. The second seeks to properly document the rear deck and patio that
was constructed approximately forty years ago.

b. The shed setback variance allows a deteriorating preexisting metal shed with
a more suitable one. It also remedies a hardship caused by an odd-shaped
lot.

c. The front yard storage variance will not alter the character of the
neighborhood, as it is intended to blend the preexisting trash storage with the
existing fence. An arbor vitae has been planted in front of the enclosure that
will all but hide it when mature. Many residences in this neighborhood have
refuse containers visible from the street. This variance will reduce that
number by one.

2. The spirit of the ordinance is preserved because:

a. Decks:

1. The front deck is consistent with residences in the immediate area.

2. The rear deck is not visible to any abutters at ground level and is
behind a solid fence. In the case of interior lots, this is common for
both the interior lot and the lots behind it.

b. Adeteriorating metal shed will remain if the variance is not granted. It is :
an eyesore, particularly to abutters Trepanier and Stefanik from their
upper floors. Because the shed is not separated by much from the
dwelling, a condition of the variance should be that no liquid fuel be stored
in the shed.

c. The storage box provides a solution to a lot-length problem while blending
with the adjacent fence.

3. Substantial justice would be done because:

a. The deck variance would allow fair use of the front and rear yards that are
severely limited by the shape of the lot.

b. The shed replacement would replace an existing eyesore visible to 2
abutters from their upper floors. In particular, a slightly taller and 2-foot
deeper shed will remove the current need to store some materials on the
roof of the shed.

c. The trash storage box would provide relief for the applicants due to the
shape of their lot and the distance from the side of the dwelling to the
street. The distance from the street to the box is greater than most of the
nearby residents have from the street to the side of their dwelling.

4. The value of surrounding properties would not be diminished because:
a. The decks increase property values because
1. The front deck provides a clean, comfortable place to sit and view
the front garden, while improving access to the front door.
===== page 14 =====
2. The rear decking was untreated, painted wood that was often rotted
and unsightly. The treated framing was retained, and the decking
was replaced with Azek composite decking. The adjacent patio area
was concrete and gravel clad with dated plastic grid panels that
followed the slope of the concrete. Those were replaced with Azek
composite that is now furred just enough to level the patio. This
creates a modern, attractive rear yard, constructed with high-value
materials, creating what many visitors have described as an oasis
in the middle of the block.

b. Would replace an eyesore with a modern shed. A Reeds Ferry American
Classic shed is anticipated as the replacement.

c. Would improve the neighborhood by placing one set of trash containers
out of the public view.

5. Literal enforcement of the provisions of the ordinance would result in
unnecessary hardship if:
(B) Owing to the following special conditions of the property that
distinguish it from other properties in the area, the property cannot be
reasonably used in strict conformance with the ordinance, and a variance
is therefore necessary to enable a reasonable use of it.

a. The lot is one of a very few interior lots remaining in Manchester. Unlike
the vast majority of single-family lots in the R-1, R-2, and MX-1 zones, the
rear of the house is within feet of the current setback requirement. For
over sixty years, there has been a concrete and gravel patio.

In 2004, the late husband of the current homeowner drew a permit for a
deck that would encroach into the side setback. At the time, there was a
stipulation that a variance would be sought upon completion of the project.
Unfortunately, he passed away without having told his widow about the
variance requirement.

Over the years, the existing rear deck leading to a concrete and gravel
patio has been repaired numerous times and was finally re-clad in
composite decking over the original framing. The concrete patio was also
covered in composite boards at grade, with only as much furring as
needed to level it. Some leftover decking was also used to level and cover
an adjacent patch of gravel for the homeowner’s gas grill. If the variance is
not granted, the homeowner will lose fair use of a comfortable deck and
patio. This request seeks to fulfill an oversight regarding the original 2004
permit and to document a preexisting deck and patio that dated back to
the 1980s.

b. The preexisting shed has outlived its usefulness. Its roof has been
covered, its door replaced, and its walls patched, but it continues to
===== page 15 =====
deteriorate. Its replacement requires a variance due to setback issues
common to the interior lot design. There is literally no location in the rear
yard that will not encroach on a setback. A slightly taller and 2-foot deeper
shed is desired to give capacity to clean up that part of the yard.
If the variance is granted, the two abutters, Stefanic and Trepanier, who
can see it from their upper floors, will have a more pleasant view, and the
homeowner will not find it necessary to store some garden items on the
shed roof. It is expected that the shed will be replaced within the next year.
c. Literal enforcement of the ordinance regarding placement of refuse
containers would require that they be placed behind the front of the
dwelling. As can be seen from the attached Google Maps photo and
boundary survey, that would mean a trip greater than half the width of the
block, some of which is over lawn areas. There is also a setback issue for
the storage container.
For decades, there have been various commercial plastic storage
containers used to hide the refuse containers at the current location. The
latest one has been replaced by a wooden one painted to match the
surrounding fence corner. The homeowner is requesting a variance
because this is a replacement for the preexisting storage container. In an
area where the majority of trash bins are visible from the street, this
variance would reduce that number by one.
