Variance Criteria Narrative
1. Not Contrary to Public Interest
Granting this variance will not alter the essential character of the neighborhood or threaten
public health, safety, or welfare. The proposed second driveway is modest in scale, located
entirely on my property, and consistent with typical residential parking patterns in the
surrounding area, where many homes accommodate multiple vehicles through widened
driveways or expanded parking areas. It will improve on-site parking availability and reduce
reliance on street parking, supporting safer traffic flow and clearer access for emergency
vehicles. The driveway will be constructed to meet all applicable safety and design
standards, maintain proper sight lines, and avoid any hazard to vehicles or pedestrians.
Stormwater and grading will be handled appropriately to prevent adverse impacts on
abutting properties. For these reasons, the variance is not contrary to the public interest.
2. Spirit of the Ordinance is Observed
The spirit of the ordinance is to ensure safe access, prevent excessive curb cuts, and maintain
the residential character of the neighborhood. The proposed driveway respects these goals.
It does not create commercial-style parking or intensify use beyond what is permitted. The
ADU requires an additional off-street parking space, and this driveway configuration is the
most practical and safe way to meet that requirement without negatively affecting the
streetscape. The design maintains the intent of the ordinance while allowing the property to
comply with ADU parking standards.
3. Substantial Justice Is Done
Granting the variance provides a clear benefit to the property owner by enabling the ADU to
meet required off-street parking standards. Denying the variance would impose a significant
burden, as it would prevent approval of permitted residential use. There is no corresponding
harm to the public in granting the variance. When the loss to the applicant outweighs any
potential public impact, substantial justice is served.

Variance Criteria Narrative
4. Values of Surrounding Properties Would Not Be Diminished
The proposed driveway will be professionally constructed and visually consistent with the
neighborhood. It will not introduce noise, congestion, or commercial activity. Additional off-
street parking typically increases residential functionality and can enhance property
desirability. Landscaping and buffering can be added as needed to maintain curb appeal. For
these reasons, the project will not diminish surrounding property values.
5. Unnecessary Hardship
A. Special Conditions of the Property
The property has specific conditions that distinguish it from others in the area. The existing
driveway cannot be expanded or connected due to city requirements, and the lot
configuration limits where additional parking can be safely placed. Because the ADU requires
dedicated off-street parking, strict application of the ordinance prevents the property from
meeting a requirement tied directly to a permitted use.
i. No Fair and Substantial Relationship
There is no fair and substantial relationship between the general purpose of the driveway
spacing ordinance and its application to this property. The ordinance aims to prevent unsafe
curb cuts and traffic conflicts, but the proposed driveway is modest, safe, and does not create
congestion or hazards. Applying the ordinance strictly would prevent a reasonable
residential use without advancing the ordinance's purpose.
ii. Reasonable Use
The proposed driveway is a reasonable use. It supports the ADU's required parking, reduces
reliance on street parking, and is consistent with typical residential improvements. It does
not intensify use beyond what zoning allows and is necessary to enable a permitted use of
the property.
